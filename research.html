Astrophysics

Accretion and outflows in black hole X-ray binaries

Student Research Assistant · Supervisor: Dr. Alex Tetarenko

When a massive star in a binary collapses into a black hole, it can begin pulling material from its companion. That gas spirals inward through a hot accretion disk that shines in X-rays, while some of it is launched outward in jets seen at radio wavelengths. How those jets form and how much energy they carry are still open questions.

I work with 45 measurement sets from the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA), processed through automated Python pipelines. Using radio interferometry techniques (manual calibration, data flagging and continuum subtraction), I isolate faint molecular emission lines from atmospheric artifacts. In CASA I apply spatial masks and run iterative deconvolution (tclean) to build 3D spectral cubes, then analyse them in CARTA with moment maps to track target emission and verify non-detections.

I also maintain a local database of more than 100 ALMA observations, which makes it much faster to cross-reference spectral windows, frequencies and target fields for multi-epoch analysis.

AI & Machine Learning

Computer vision for behavioural neuroscience

Student Research Assistant · Supervisor: Dr. Hardeep Ryait

Developed a Python computer-vision pipeline using YOLO and OpenCV to automate behavioural tracking and spatial analysis of lab mice in real-time video. Engineered custom polygon zone mapping and identity logic across more than 1,000 frames, accelerated with Metal Performance Shaders (MPS).

Built an interactive tool for video selection and frame-by-frame annotation to speed up dataset development, with ground-truth labels exported simultaneously in COCO JSON, YOLO and Pascal VOC formats to keep the data consistent for interdisciplinary neuroscience research.

  • Python
  • YOLO
  • OpenCV
  • Dataset annotation

Theoretical Physics

Black holes, relativity and neutrino phenomenology

Research Assistant, Theoretical Physics Group · Supervisor: Dr. Saurya Das

Took part in group seminars on quantum mechanics and cosmology. Carried out directed studies of stellar evolution, gravitational collapse and black hole dynamics, followed by literature reviews of the geometric formalism of special relativity (the Minkowski metric) and the classic experimental tests of general relativity, later extending to a foundational study of quantum gravity candidates.

Assisted with analysis of how the Generalized Uncertainty Principle induces observable dips in ultrahigh-energy neutrino spectra, reviewed evidence for sterile neutrino signatures, and analysed how matter-enhanced oscillations (the MSW effect) influence neutrino survival probabilities in dense environments.

  • General relativity
  • Cosmology
  • Quantum gravity
  • Neutrino physics

Quantum Computing

Quantum algorithms and circuit simulation

Student Research Assistant · Supervisor: Dr. Daya Gaur

Studied quantum information fundamentals from Nielsen & Chuang, deriving the matrix representations of single- and multi-qubit gates by hand. Implemented and simulated key algorithms, including Deutsch–Jozsa, Shor’s and HHL, using Qiskit and IBM cloud quantum processors.

Produced a comprehensive set of textbook solutions and working circuits demonstrating quantum teleportation and arithmetic operations.
